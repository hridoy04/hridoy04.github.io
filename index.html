Rubayet Alam

I study how viruses change enough to slip past immunity, and I build ways to spot those changes before they spread.

Evolutionary virologist and PhD researcher at the MRC–University of Glasgow Centre for Virus Research. Over ten years across molecular virology, genomics and computational biology.

What I’m working on

Predicting antigenic escape from a single sequence

A new approach that uses protein language models to predict how a virus’s antigenicity could change. I tested it on WHO priority viruses, including the recently emerged Ebola Bundibugyo and Andes hantavirus.

Presented at the Microbiology Society Annual Conference in Belfast and at the Computational Biology Symposium in Glasgow, 2026.

Norovirus GII.4, mapped by sequence

I applied the same approach to human norovirus GII.4 in different clinical states, then ran Bayesian phylogenetic and ancestral-state reconstruction analyses. Along the way I built a sequence-based antigenic cartography to map the variants.

Why some variants win in some places

Using SARS-CoV-2 as the model, I combine epidemiological and genomic data to study regional immune-distinctiveness and whether it helps explain which variants succeed.
